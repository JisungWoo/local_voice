Antelope Canyon looks like waves frozen in stone. So how did solid rock end up looking like this?

The story starts with sand, not a canyon.

Long before these passages existed, wind piled sand into enormous desert dunes.

Over time, those dunes were buried. Pressure packed the grains together, and minerals in groundwater glued them into sandstone.

Much later, flash floods began carving through that rock.

And the water carried a tool of its own: sand.

Swept against the walls, those grains acted like sandpaper, helping wear the rock away.

Flood after flood, the passages grew deeper, and sharp edges became flowing curves.

So when you walk through Antelope Canyon, you're walking through ancient sand dunes, sculpted by water.
